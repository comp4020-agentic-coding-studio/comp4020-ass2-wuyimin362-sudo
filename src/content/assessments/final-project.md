## The brief

> Account for your semester as a decomposition rather than a narrative: three
> named mechanisms, the positions they took money from, and what is left over.

"What is left over" is the interesting phrase. Total your losses, attribute what
you can to named mechanisms, and the residue — the part that was the market, or
the part you cannot explain — gets reported as a residue rather than absorbed
into a story.

## Three distinct mechanisms

Three, from the eleven the course named, each traced to a position your log can
evidence.

Distinct means distinct. Concentration and correlation collapse are both about
what happens when one thing fails, and a report that treats them as two labels
for the same event has supplied one mechanism, not two. If you cannot say what
would have had to be different for mechanism B to have not applied while A still
did, they are the same mechanism.

Six of the eleven can be committed deliberately and are therefore always
available: cost drag, concentration, return chasing, story, overfitting, loss
aversion. The other five have to be handed to you. If the semester handed you
one, use it and say so; if it did not, say that too. **A report that claims a
rationed mechanism it cannot evidence is the one failure the marking does not
forgive**, because the entire premise is that the numbers happened to the person
who wrote them.

## The closing section

The last part of the account re-reads your [opening record](/records/) — all four
answers, quoted verbatim, from the sheet you filed in the first session and were
not permitted to revise.

For each answer, two sentences: what you knew when you wrote it, and which week
of the course took it apart. Then one paragraph on the week 1 lecture, which you
have now heard twice, read against your own ledger position and the class's.

The question that paragraph answers is not "was the course right". It is
narrower: **what would have to be true about your semester for you to be able to
tell the difference between having judged well and having been one of the
thirteen?** Most accounts conclude that nothing in twelve weeks could establish
it. That is an acceptable conclusion and the arithmetic supports it. What is not
acceptable is not noticing that the question applies to you.

## What you submit

A single PDF, 2000 to 3000 words, plus the complete
[position log](/records/) for the semester and your opening record as an
appendix. If you photographed the record in week 1, the photograph is fine.

## How it is read

Not on the outcome. A student who finished up 40% and can account for three
mechanisms out of their own log does well; a student who lost everything and
cannot evidence why does not. The [class ledger](/sessions/04-closing-crit/) is
read out in the last session and then plays no part in the marking, which is
consistent with what the course spent twelve weeks arguing: the number at the
end is one sample, and the decomposition is the only part that transfers.

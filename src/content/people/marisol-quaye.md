Marisol convenes this course and lectures weeks 1, 2, 5, 9 and 12 — the ones
where the point is a derivation rather than a decision.

They wrote it after several years of teaching an ordinary introduction to markets
and noticing that students left it able to describe risk and unable to recognise
it. The material was not wrong. It was arranged so that every mechanism arrived
as a caveat attached to something appealing, which is a reliable way to produce
someone who can define a margin call and will still be surprised by one.

So this version removes the appealing part. Twelve mechanisms, each with its
arithmetic, each checked against a position the student is actually holding, and
no week in which the course implies that any of this can be turned to your
advantage. They are aware of how that reads in a prospectus and consider it the
honest version.

Ask them about the [bounded-loss policy](/policies/) if you are deciding between
the paper and real-money tracks, and email them rather than waiting for a
session if a position has begun to affect your sleep or your finances. That is
the part of the policy they care most about being taken literally.

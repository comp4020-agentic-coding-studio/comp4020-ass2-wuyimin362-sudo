This is the week 1 lecture. The slides are the same file. Nothing has been added
to them and nothing has been taken out.

The bet paid 4.5% per go and ruined 87 people in 100, and in February that was
an argument about a bet nobody had offered you. Today it is read in a room where
the [class ledger](/sessions/04-closing-crit/) is on the screen and roughly a
third of you finished the semester with more than you started with.

Those people are the lecture. They did not cheat and they are not fools; they
took the same twelve weeks and came out ahead, and if you ask them why, they will
tell you something specific and true about what they chose. Week 1's arithmetic
says what that explanation is worth: of a hundred people running the same
exposure, thirteen finish holding forty-five times their stake, and each of them
has a sincere account of their own judgement. The account is not evidence.
Neither is the money.

The symmetrical point is the one that costs more to accept. **Your own loss is
not evidence either.** You are one path. The ten weeks in between are what let
you say something stronger than "I lost" — you can name which mechanism took
each dollar, show the size you chose, the friction you paid, the alternative you
wrote down before you knew how it ended. That is not a story about your
judgement. It is a decomposition, and it is the only thing in this course that
survives contact with somebody else's twelve weeks.

So there is no new material today, and there is no reassuring coda either. The
course has one claim, it made it in February, and the reason it is made twice is
that the first time it was information and the second time it is a description of
something in the room.

Close your positions. Then write the [final project](/assessments/final-project/):
three mechanisms, traced to positions you actually held, and a last section that
re-reads the four answers you filed in the first session without revising them.

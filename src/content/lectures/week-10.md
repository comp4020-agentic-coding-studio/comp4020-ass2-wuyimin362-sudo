Read question 4 of your [opening record](/records/) — what would make you close
it — and then read your [position log](/records/) beside it.

Two dates. **The date you closed something that was up.** And, for anything you
are still holding at a loss, **the date you bought it.** Count the days between
that second date and today.

The pattern almost everyone finds is that the first number is small and the
second is large: the winners were sold within weeks, the losers have been held
since March. This is the **disposition effect** — the tendency to realise gains
early and defer losses — and it follows from something that sounds harmless: a
loss of a given size feels bigger than a gain of the same size. If closing at a
loss is what makes the loss real to you, then not closing feels like not having
lost, and the position stays open for reasons that have nothing to do with the
position.

Notice what this does in combination with the rest of the course. A held loser is
a concentration decision you are making again every morning without recording it
(week 3), and it is a position whose original reason you have probably not
re-read since week 1 (week 7).

You are not being asked to accept this from a study. It is in your log, with
dates you wrote, and that is the only reason it is a lecture rather than a claim.
If your log shows the opposite pattern, bring it — that is a finding too, and a
rarer one.

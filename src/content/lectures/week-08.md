Read question 3 of your [opening record](/records/): how many positions, and
what do they have in common.

Splitting a position is the standard answer to week 3. Two holdings instead of
one, so a failure in either costs you half as much. That works when the two
things fail for different reasons, and it does nothing when they fail for the
same reason.

Price it. Split $10,980 into two positions of $5,490. One falls 33%, the other
falls 29%:

```
$5,490 × 0.67  =  $3,678
$5,490 × 0.71  =  $3,898
                  ───────
                  $7,576
```

Against having left it all in the first one:

```
$10,980 × 0.67  =  $7,357
```

**The diversification was worth $219, or 2.0% of the position.** Two decisions,
two sets of fees, and about two percent of protection, because both things sold
into the same market and fell for the same reason in the same fortnight.

**Correlation** is the degree to which two things move together, and the number
people quote for it is measured over calm periods, because that is most of the
data. The protection you are buying is only needed during the other periods, and
during those the correlation of almost everything rises toward one — partly
because the same holders are selling everything they own at once to raise cash,
and your two positions are in the same portfolios as each other.

So the diversification is real in the months you do not need it and thin in the
weeks you do. Compute your own $219 this week: what your split actually saved,
against the counterfactual of not having split.

Your paper account has been lying to you since week 1, and it was the course
that arranged the lie. Today it gets corrected, which is why this lecture sits
immediately before you defend your arithmetic.

## Three things your account pretends

When you press sell, the paper platform fills you **immediately**, in **whatever
quantity you asked for**, at **the price on the screen**. All three are false
about real markets, and the third is the one that hides the other two.

A quote is not a price. A quote is an offer to trade a **stated quantity** at a
price — someone will buy 300 units from you at $39.96, and that is the entire
extent of their commitment. Sell 300 and you get $39.96. Sell 3,000 and the
first 300 go at $39.96 and the rest go to whoever is next, at worse prices,
until either you are done or the buyers are. The number on the screen was true
for the first tenth of your position.

So the price at which you can leave depends on how much you are leaving with.
Nothing in the previous four weeks acknowledged this. Every round trip we
computed, every counterfactual you filed, every closing value in your log was
priced as though your size did not exist.

## And the quantity is not constant

The quoted size is not a fixed property of the instrument. It is a handful of
people choosing, this morning, to stand ready to trade. When the instrument
becomes frightening, some of them stop, and the quantity available at any
sensible price shrinks — at precisely the moment a large number of holders all
want to leave.

This is the shape of the failure: **not that the price fell, but that the exit
you had priced into your plan was not there when you went to use it.** Your
position was real. The door was a quote.

## Mark your own record

Go through your [position log](/records/) and add one line at the top:

> Every price in this log assumes a fill that is instant, complete, and at the
> quoted price. None of those is guaranteed.

That asterisk applies retroactively to weeks 2 through 5. It does not make those
numbers wrong — the spread you paid was real, the $12.00 was real — it makes
them a floor. The real versions are worse and by an amount your account cannot
show you.

## This one is not on the assignment

You cannot commit this failure on purpose. Concentration is a number you choose;
return chasing is a decision you make; cost drag happens the moment you
transact. Illiquidity has to be handed to you by the world, it cannot happen at
all on a paper account, and on a $20 real position you are far too small for
anyone to notice.

So it is taught and not assessed, and if this semester does not hand it to
anyone, nobody's mark changes. Write "not experienced this semester" in your log
and mean it. Knowing which of your losses were available to you and which were
rationed is itself part of reading your own record honestly — there are four
more of these coming, and week 11 is the one nobody gets.

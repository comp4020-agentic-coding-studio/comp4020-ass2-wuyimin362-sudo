By now something in your field of view has gone up more than the thing you own.
This lecture is about what happens next, and the honest version of the lecture
is that many of you will do it this morning and name it this afternoon.

## The worked example

Follow the arithmetic; the instruments are labels, not recommendations.

You opened with 250 units of **A** at $40.00, which is $10,000, plus $5 to
transact. Four weeks later A is at $44.00, so the position is worth

```
250 × $44.00  =  $11,000
```

Meanwhile **B** has gone from $10.00 to $18.00. It has risen 80% while yours
rose 10%. You rotate. Sell A at the bid of $43.96:

```
250 × $43.96   =  $10,990
  less $5 fee  =  $10,985
```

and buy B at the ask of $18.00:

```
$10,980 / $18.00  =  610 units,  plus $5 fee
```

You now hold 610 units of B. Note in passing that you have just paid the week 2
round trip again — about $20 — for the privilege of changing your mind.

## What was the evidence

Write down, in one sentence, why B and not A.

For most people the sentence reduces to: **B went up more.** Not that B sells
more of something, or costs less relative to what it earns, or has customers who
cannot easily leave. That it went up. The move is the evidence and the move is
the reason.

Recent performance is a real fact about the past and a weak claim about what
happens next. Acting on it has a name — this week's name — and it is the most
common way a position is destroyed by its owner rather than by the market.

## Fill in the counterfactual, today

From today your [position log](/records/) carries one more column, and it is
filled in **on the day of the action**:

> If I do nothing today, what happens?

For the example above that entry reads: *250 units of A, worth $11,000.* That is
it. No forecast, no reasoning — the position you would still be holding and its
value at a price you can check.

This matters because of what it becomes. Carry the example to week 12, where A
is $46.00 and B is $14.00:

```
what you chose:     610 × $14.00  =  $8,540
what you refused:   250 × $46.00  =  $11,500
                                     ─────────
gap                                  $2,960
```

A rose 15% over the period. **The market was not the problem.** The $2,960 is
the price of one decision made on one kind of evidence, and it is separable from
everything the market did because you wrote the alternative down before you knew
how it ended.

Filled in on the day, that column is evidence. Reconstructed in week 11 it is a
story about yourself, and nobody writes an unflattering one. This is the single
mechanical reason the column exists.

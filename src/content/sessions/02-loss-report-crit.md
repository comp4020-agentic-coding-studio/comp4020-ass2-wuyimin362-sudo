Bring the log. Not a summary of the log, not slides about the log — the rows,
with the prices you actually transacted at.

## Before the session

Pick your mode. [Assignment 1](/assessments/assignment-1/) accepts one of three:
cost drag, concentration, or return chasing. Have the arithmetic with you, and
have it as arithmetic — an opening position, what happened, a closing position,
and the subtraction that connects them.

If your chosen mode is return chasing, you need the counterfactual entry you
filed on the day. If you did not file one, pick a different mode; a
reconstruction written this week is not admissible and pretending otherwise
wastes the crit.

## In the session

Four-minute reports, in pairs, then questions from the room rather than from the
tutor. Each report answers three things and nothing else: what you did, what it
cost, and which named mode accounts for the gap.

Then everyone gets the same question, because this morning's lecture handed it
over: **what would it have cost you to actually get out?** Your log prices assume
an instant complete fill at the quote. Say by how much you think that flatters
your number, and say that you cannot know.

## Afterwards

You have two weeks and a mid-semester break before the report is due. What
usually changes after this session is not the arithmetic but the choice of mode —
about a third of the room discovers that the loss they planned to write about is
not the one their log can actually evidence.

That discovery is the point of holding the crit before the deadline rather than
after it.

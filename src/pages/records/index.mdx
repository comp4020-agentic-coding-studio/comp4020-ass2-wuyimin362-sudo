# Records

Two documents. You fill the first one once and never change it. You add to the
second one every time you do anything.

Both are assessed, and both are read back to you in later weeks, so write them
for a reader who is not you.

## The opening record

Filed in the first session, before you open anything. Four questions. Answer
them in the order they appear and do not revise them afterwards — a later
revision destroys the only thing this document is for.

**1. What are you buying, and how much of your account goes into it?**

Name the instrument and give the position as a percentage of your account
balance. If you are opening more than one position, list each with its
percentage.

**2. Why this one? One sentence.**

Not a paragraph. One sentence, in your own words, in the form "I am buying it
because ___". Whatever you write here is what you actually knew at the time,
which is the reason you are writing it before you buy rather than after.

**3. How many positions do you hold, and what do they have in common?**

If the answer to the second half is "nothing", write "nothing". If two of them
sell the same thing, or serve the same customers, or rise and fall with the same
input, say so.

**4. What would make you close it?**

A price, a date, a condition, or the word "nothing". "Nothing" is an acceptable
answer and a common one. It is not a wrong answer in week 1.

File it, and do not look at it again until you are asked to.

## The position log

One row per action, added on the day of the action. An action is opening,
adding, reducing, closing, or a fee being charged. Not a thought — an action.

Every row carries:

| Field | What goes in it |
| --- | --- |
| Date | The day the action happened |
| Action | open / add / reduce / close / fee |
| Instrument | What it was |
| Units | How many |
| Price | The price you actually transacted at, not the last price you saw |
| Side of the quote | Whether you paid the ask or received the bid |
| Fee | In dollars, including anything the platform calls something else |
| Account after | Your balance once this action settled |

The fifth and sixth fields are the ones people leave out, and they are the ones
week 2 is about. If you record "bought at $40" when the quote was $39.96 bid and
$40.00 ask, you have thrown away the cost you paid to get in, and it is the only
cost in this course you are guaranteed to pay.

### The counterfactual column

From week 4 onwards the log carries one more column, and it has to be filled in
**on the day of the action, not afterwards**:

> **If I do nothing today, what happens?**

Write the position you would still be holding and what it is worth right now.
That is all. You are not predicting anything; you are recording the thing you
chose against, at the moment you chose against it, at a price you can check.

Filled in on the day, this column is evidence. Filled in later, it is a story
about yourself, and everyone tells themselves a flattering one. In week 12 you
will be asked to total it.

## Arithmetic

Every number in an assessment has to be reproducible from numbers you also
supply. "The position lost about a fifth of its value" is not a finding. This
is:

```
open:   250 units at $40.00 ask   = $10,000.00
fee                               =      $5.00
close:  250 units at $39.96 bid   =  $9,990.00
fee                               =      $5.00
round trip                        =     $20.00  (0.20% of $10,000)
```

Four lines and a subtraction. Do that, every time, and the week 12 account
writes itself.

# Cost drag

Week 2

## Nothing happened last week

## And you are already down

---

## The quote had two prices

You saw one number. There were two.

**bid** — someone will buy from you

**ask** — someone will sell to you

The ask is higher.

---

## Say the quote was

```
$39.96  bid
$40.00  ask
```

You paid the ask.

Change your mind one second later and you receive the bid.

---

## So at the instant you bought

```
$40.00 − $39.96  =  $0.04

$0.04 / $40.00   =  0.10%
```

No news. Nobody was wrong about anything.

---

## Then the platform charges you

```
$5 in  +  $5 out   =  $10

$10 / $10,000      =  0.10%
```

---

## One round trip

```
0.10%  +  0.10%  =  0.20%
```

**$20 on $10,000.**

In and out. No opinion about the market.

---

{/* _class: centered */}

## We just did this

Whole room, fifty minutes, open and close.

**Check your account.**

---

## Twenty dollars is not interesting

What makes it week 2 is that it **recurs**.

And recurring costs compound against you

exactly the way returns compound for you.

---

## Leave it alone for forty years

Total drag 1% per year:

```
0.99^40  =  0.669
```

---

{/* _class: impact */}

# 66.9%

A third of it is gone.

---

## Not to a crash

Not to a bad decision.

Not to anything that appeared in a newspaper.

To a number so small it is usually quoted as reassurance.

---

## Now use your own number

Four round trips a year, at the 0.20% we measured:

```
4 × 0.20%   =  0.80% per year

0.992^40    =  0.725
```

You keep 72.5%.

---

# Leverage

Week 5

---

{/* _class: centered */}

## Every other week, you go and do the thing

## This week you may not

---

## The policy

Real-money positions are **long-only and fully paid**.

You own the thing. The most you can lose is what you paid.

That rule exists because of one number.

---

## Start with last week's position

```
610 units of B at $18.00  =  $10,980
```

Your own money.

---

## Now borrow the same again

```
1,220 units at $18.00  =  $21,960

   your money          =  $10,980
   borrowed            =  $10,980
```

---

## Your equity

What is left after repaying the loan:

```
equity  =  1,220P − $10,980
```

At $18.00 that is $10,980. As it should be.

---

## The lender is watching one number

**Maintenance margin.** Keep equity above some

fraction of the position's value, or it is closed for you.

Take 25%.

---

## Solve for the price

```
(1,220P − 10,980) / 1,220P  =  0.25

     1,220P − 10,980        =  305P

                     915P   =  10,980
```

---

{/* _class: impact */}

# P = $12.00

---

## Not a price you chose

Not a price you were warned about.

Not a price at which you have any say.

Three inputs, one of them a fraction in a document you did not read.

---

## Now suppose B does this

```
$18.00  →  $11.50  →  $14.00
```

---

## Unleveraged

You hold 610 units the whole way.

```
610 × $14.00  =  $8,540
```

Down. **And still holding.**

---

## Leveraged

Closed out at $12.00 on the way down.

```
1,220 × $12.00 − $10,980  =  $3,660
```

The move from $12 to $14 happened to somebody else.

---

## But you would have guessed

B fell 22.2%. Twice the exposure, twice the loss:

```
$10,980 × (1 − 2 × 0.222)  =  $6,101
```

You have **$3,660**.

---

{/* _class: impact */}

# The missing $2,441

is not the size of the fall.

It is the shape of it.

---

## It happens without borrowing too

```
0.50 × 1.50  =  0.75
```

Down 25% after two moves that sound like they cancel.

To get back from 0.50 to 1.00 the price needs **+100%**, not +50%.

---

## And if B keeps falling

```
1,220P  =  $10,980   →   P = $9.00
```

At $9.00 your equity is zero.

---

{/* _class: impact */}

# Below $9.00 you owe money

you never deposited.

---

## At $8.00

```
position   1,220 × $8.00  =   $9,760
loan                      =  $10,980
                             ─────────
you owe                        $1,220
```

On top of having lost everything you put in.

---

## Unleveraged, at $9.00

```
610 × $9.00  =  $5,490
```

A bad week. **And yours.**

---

{/* _class: centered */}

## A $20 cap is not a cap

## if the instrument can send you a bill

So we derive the $12.00, and forbid you from paying to experience it.

# Return chasing

Week 4

---

{/* _class: centered */}

## Something in your field of view

## has gone up more than yours

---

## You opened with

```
250 units of A at $40.00  =  $10,000
fee                       =      $5
```

---

## Four weeks later

```
A  =  $44.00
250 × $44.00  =  $11,000
```

Up 10%. Fine.

---

## Meanwhile

```
B:  $10.00  →  $18.00
```

**Up 80%.**

---

## So you rotate

```
sell A at bid $43.96   250 × $43.96  =  $10,990
                              less fee      $5
                                        ───────
                                        $10,985

buy B at ask $18.00    $10,980 / $18  =  610 units
                              plus fee      $5
```

---

{/* _class: centered */}

## You also just paid the week 2 round trip again

**$20**, for the privilege of changing your mind.

---

{/* _class: impact */}

# What was the evidence?

One sentence. Write it now.

---

## For most people it reduces to

{/* _class: quote */}

### B went up more.

---

## Not that it

sells more of something

costs less relative to what it earns

has customers who cannot easily leave

---

## Recent performance is

a **real fact** about the past

a **weak claim** about what happens next

---

## Fill in the counterfactual

Today. Not in April.

> If I do nothing today, what happens?

---

## For this example

```
250 units of A, worth $11,000
```

No forecast. No reasoning.

The position you would still hold, at a price you can check.

---

## Carry it to week 12

```
A  =  $46.00        B  =  $14.00

chose:    610 × $14.00  =   $8,540
refused:  250 × $46.00  =  $11,500
                           ─────────
gap                          $2,960
```

---

{/* _class: impact */}

# A rose 15%

The market was not the problem.

---

## On the day, that column is evidence

Reconstructed in week 11, it is a story about yourself.

Nobody writes an unflattering one.

---

{/* _class: centered */}

## Do it today and you have an assignment

Do it in April and you have an anecdote.

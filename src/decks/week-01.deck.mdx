# Ruin

Week 1

---

## Here is a bet

Nineteen times in twenty, it pays you **10%**.

One time in twenty, it takes **everything** you staked.

---

## What is it worth per go

```
(19/20) × 1.10  +  (1/20) × 0

=  0.95 × 1.10

=  1.045
```

**+4.5% per bet.**

---

## Take it forty times

```
1.045^40  =  5.82
```

Nearly **six times** your money.

---

{/* _class: centered */}

## That is a good bet

Every number on the last two slides is correct.

---

## Do not take it

To finish forty bets, you have to survive forty bets.

---

## The chance of that

```
(19/20)^40  =  0.95^40  =  0.1285
```

---

{/* _class: impact */}

# 13 in 100

The other 87 are at zero.

---

## Zero is not a bad year

It is the end of the sequence.

Nothing multiplies back up out of nothing.

---

## Both numbers are true

100 people. 40 bets each.

```
between them, they end with   582
still holding anything         12.85
```

---

## What each survivor holds

```
1.10^40  =  45.26
```

A survivor is someone the loss never happened to.

So every survivor multiplied by 1.10 **exactly forty times**.

---

{/* _class: centered */}

## They all hold the identical amount

There is no spread among the winners to explain.

---

## The thirteen will be asked how

They will have answers.

The answers will be specific, sincere, and worthless —

the eighty-seven did the same thing.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

## Gambler's ruin

The name is old. The arithmetic is all of it.

---

## The claim, narrowly

Take a bet with a chance of total loss **repeatedly**,

and expected value stops deciding your outcome.

**How many times you are exposed** decides it.

---

## Take it a hundred times instead

```
survival   0.95^100   =  0.59%

average    1.045^100  =  81.59×
```

---

{/* _class: impact */}

# Both got worse

The bet is 14× more attractive.

You are 22× less likely to be there.

---

## Same act, both consequences

Accepting it more often.

There is no version of this where the average is the thing to look at.
